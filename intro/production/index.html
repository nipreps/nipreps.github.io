    Scientific production¶

    This page indexes the scientific output of the NiPreps community since 2016: peer-reviewed papers and preprints, conference communications, the community's presence at the annual meeting of the Organization for Human Brain Mapping (OHBM), talks and educational events, and the releases of its software. To cite a NiPreps tool in your work, follow the citation boilerplate that each tool writes with its outputs (see Transparency of workflows).

    Is something missing?

    This index is maintained by hand. If a paper, abstract, talk or event about NiPreps is missing, please edit this page and send a pull request.

    NiPreps at OHBM, 2023 to 2026¶

    The OHBM annual meeting is where the NiPreps community meets every year: at the OHBM BrainHack before the meeting, in educational courses and symposia, and in the poster halls.

    2026, Bordeaux¶

    • BrainHack: the NiPreps TrainTrack, June 11 (slides), and "BIDS @ BrainHack 2026" (slides).
    • Educational courses: two talks on June 14, "Compute Once, Reuse Forever: Sustainable Science with NiPreps" (slides) and "Using Large Language Models (LLMs) in Neuroimaging Research: LLMs for Coding" (slides).
    • Recognition: the OHBM Open Science Award to Chris Markiewicz, NiPreps co-founder and Steering Committee member.

    2025, Brisbane¶

    • Abstract: Garcia M et al. MRIQC IQM variability and artifact prediction in multi-echo fMRI. doi:10.31219/osf.io/6krp9_v1

    2024, Seoul¶

    • BrainHack: NiPreps sponsored the BrainHack and led projects on fMRIPrep, NiTransforms, SDCFlows and multi-echo MRIQC (slides).
    • Symposium: "fMRIPrep-Infants: Bringing standardized, easy-to-use, transparent preprocessing to the infant MRI space" (M. Goncalves), in the symposium "In the beginning... Advances in fetal and infant imaging".
    • Abstracts:
      • Provins C et al. Effects of phase-encoding on BOLD data with a positive control task (#1361). doi:10.31219/osf.io/nuqs2
      • Markiewicz C et al. FMRIPrep-next: Preprocessing as a fit-transform model (#2257).
      • Esteban O et al. SDCFlows: convergent formalization of MRI field mappings for reliable distortion correction (#1692).

    2023, Montréal¶

    • BrainHack: NiPreps joined the OHBM BrainHack in place of its own first hackathon.
    • Educational course: "Quality Control in fMRI studies with MRIQC and fMRIPrep" (C. Provins, slides).
    • Abstracts:
      • Goncalves M et al. They grow up so fast: Augmenting the NiBabies infant MRI workflow.
      • Provins C et al. Signal-to-noise ratio estimates predict head motion presence in T1-weighted MRI. doi:10.31219/osf.io/7vqzr
      • Provins C et al. Assessment of B1 field dynamics of rats BOLD fMRI using the wavelet transform. doi:10.31219/osf.io/hnpyt
      • Savary E et al. Q'kay: a manager for the quality assessment of large neuroimaging studies. doi:10.31219/osf.io/edx6t
      • Alemán-Gómez Y et al. CHIMERA: An open source framework for combining multiple parcellations.

    Earlier, at OHBM 2022: educational session "Executing BIDS-Apps on large datasets and the NiPreps framework" (slides).

    Milestone releases¶

    • fMRIPrep 1.0.0 (December 7, 2017): the first stable release.
    • fMRIPrep 20.2.0 (September 28, 2020): the first long-term support (LTS) series.
    • NiReports 23.0.0 (March 10, 2023): visual reports move into their own library.
    • fMRIPrep 23.2.0 (January 10, 2024): the fit & transform refactor, with the --level and --derivatives options to reuse precomputed results.
    • MRIQC 24.0.0 (April 17, 2024): quality control of diffusion MRI.
    • PETPrep 0.0.2 (September 16, 2025): the first release.
    • fMRIPrep 25.2.0 (October 2, 2025): the second long-term support series.
    • TemplateFlow client 25.1.0 (October 21, 2025): a new client interface.
    • NiFreeze 0.1.0 (February 6, 2026): the first release.
    • MRIQC 26.0.0 (November 5, 2026): the first release of the 26.x series.
